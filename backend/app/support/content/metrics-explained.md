## Sales and money

- Revenue: total sales recorded in the selected period, after returns. The percentage next to it is the change against the previous period of the same length.
- Returns: sales rows with a negative quantity are counted as returns or refunds, not sales, and are already taken off revenue. ORLA also shows gross revenue before returns.
- Gross margin: revenue minus the cost of goods sold, as a percentage of the revenue that has a known cost. It is what is left after paying for the stock you sold.
- Gross profit by product: each product's revenue minus its cost, shown in euro.
- Cost coverage: the share of revenue where a cost price was actually recorded. If it is low, margin is only an estimate.
- Tax coverage: the share of cost-known revenue that also has a VAT figure, so margin can be worked out net of VAT rather than assumed.
- Transactions and Average sale (in reports): the number of sales in the period and the average value of one sale.

## Stock

- Inventory value: the value of the stock you have now, at cost price.
- Sell-through rate: units sold divided by units sold plus units still in stock. An approximation, not an exact sell-through rate.
- Stock cover: how many days your current stock will last at the recent sales rate. Blank means there are not enough recent sales to estimate it.
- Dead stock: products with stock on hand but no sales at all in the selected period.
- Fast movers: products selling quickly, with 14 days of stock cover or less.
- Slow movers: products with 60 or more days of stock cover; candidates for a discount, a bundle or a return to the supplier.
- Inventory turnover: cost of goods sold divided by current inventory value at cost. A simplification, as it uses today's inventory value rather than an average over the period.
- Low stock: a product whose stock has fallen below its reorder point. See "Reorder rules and low-stock alerts".

## Category breakdown (in reports)

- Revenue per category: sales of products in that category.
- Expenses per category: what you paid buying stock in that category (purchase cost), not the cost of goods sold, so it differs from gross margin.
- Stock value per category: valued at selling price, not cost, so it differs from inventory value.
- Products with no category are grouped under "Uncategorized".

## Workshop

- Repairs completed: the number of repair jobs in the period.
- Average ticket: the average price charged per repair.
- Gross margin (labour only): price charged minus labour cost. Parts are not tracked yet, so this understates the true cost of repairs.

## Forecast

- Expected revenue: a projection from your own recent sales. See "The forecast".
- Suggested reorder: the upper estimate of forecast demand minus current stock. A starting point only; it does not know your supplier's delivery time or how much safety buffer you want.

## Asking ORLA about a metric

You can ask ORLA what any of these means, for example "What does sell-through mean?". Definition questions are answered straight from this list.
