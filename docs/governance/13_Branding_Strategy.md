# Important Caveat

**Nothing in this document constitutes legal advice, and no name below has been formally cleared.**

The assessments here are based on public web searches only. Before committing to any name, a proper clearance search is required through:

* **CRO** (Companies Registration Office, Ireland) — company name availability
* **IPOI** (Intellectual Property Office of Ireland) — Irish trade marks
* **EUIPO** — EU trade marks (essential, given EU-wide ambitions)
* **Domain registrars** — `.ie`, `.com`, `.eu`
* **App stores and social handles**

A trademark agent or solicitor should conduct this before any spend on logo, domain, or printed material.

# Assessment: LUMA — Not Recommended

The proposed acronyms are good (*Learn, Understand, Model, Act* / *Luminous Understanding for Market Advantage*). The name itself is the problem.

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Luma AI, Inc.** | US AI company, founded 2021, ~$1.06B raised, Series C ($900M, Nov 2025), ~332 employees |
| **Registered trademark** | "LUMA AI" filed 16 Aug 2024, serial 98703191, covering **downloadable computer software** — the same classification your product would need |
| **Domain** | lumalabs.ai established; premium variants effectively unavailable |
| **Also in use** | lu.ma (events platform), and Luma as a brand associated with Pacific Premier Bancorp (PLBC) |

**Assessment:** using LUMA for a software product would place the company in direct conflict with a very well-funded organisation holding a registered mark in the relevant class, with both the motive and the budget to enforce it. The risk is not theoretical — it is the kind of conflict that surfaces at exactly the wrong moment, such as during a funding round or an acquisition.

**Recommendation: do not proceed with LUMA.**

# Assessment: EOLAS — Also Not Recommended

Considered as an Irish-language alternative (*eolas* = "knowledge"). Unfortunately crowded:

| Existing User | Sector |
|---|---|
| Eolas Technologies Ltd (Dublin, est. 2010) | Irish IT managed services for SMEs — direct adjacency |
| Eolas International | Consumer, product and **retail intelligence** — uncomfortably close |
| Eolas Medical (Belfast) | Healthcare software |
| Eolas Designs | TCAD software |
| Eolas (Tyler, Texas, est. 1994) | US patent licensing firm with a notable litigation history |

**Assessment:** too crowded in Ireland, in software, and specifically in retail intelligence. Not recommended.

# Naming Criteria

Any candidate should meet these tests:

| Criterion | Why |
|---|---|
| **Spellable when heard** | A shop owner should be able to type it after hearing it once on a phone call |
| **Pronounceable in Ireland and internationally** | The first market is Irish; the ambition is not |
| **Short (4–6 letters ideal)** | Fits a logo, a domain, an app icon |
| **Meaningful acronym available** | Gives sales conversations a hook and reinforces the product story |
| **Not a real-word trademark minefield** | Generic dictionary words are hard to protect and usually taken |
| **Warm, not enterprise-cold** | The target user is a shop owner, not a CIO |
| **Doesn't over-index on "AI"** | AI is a component, not the product (Constitution, Principle 5). Names like "SomethingAI" age badly |
| **Doesn't over-index on bikes** | The platform is deliberately multi-industry (BD-002) |

That last point rules out otherwise-attractive cycling metaphors — **Cadence**, **Spoke**, **Drivetrain**, **Freewheel**. They are charming for bike shops and actively unhelpful when selling to a café. Several are also taken (Cadence Design Systems, FreeWheel/Comcast).

# Recommended Candidates

Presented in order of preference. **All require formal clearance before use.**

## 1. TARA

> **T**rends · **A**nalysis · **R**ecommendations · **A**ctions

The four things the product actually does, in the order it does them.

**Strengths:** genuinely Irish (the Hill of Tara, seat of the High Kings) without being hard to spell or pronounce. Four letters. Warm and human — it reads as a name, which suits a product positioned as "your business analyst." Works internationally with no pronunciation ambiguity.

**Watch for:** "Tara" is a common given name and place name, so there will be existing registrations in various classes. Clearance in software classes is essential.

## 2. NORA

> **N**umbers · **O**perations · **R**ecommendations · **A**ctions

**Strengths:** same humanising quality. Slightly softer than Tara. The acronym maps very cleanly to the product's four core outputs. Familiar in Ireland without being exclusively Irish.

**Watch for:** common name; some existing software products use it. Requires clearance.

## 3. ORLA

> **O**perational **R**eporting & **L**ogic **A**nalytics

**Strengths:** distinctly Irish (Órla), which supports the Ireland-first positioning. Four letters. Uncommon enough in software to be more likely available than Tara or Nora.

**Watch for:** the fada (Órla vs Orla) creates a spelling decision. Recommend plain "Orla" for domain and typing simplicity. Less internationally familiar.

## 4. VERA

> **V**erified **E**vidence · **R**eal **A**nswers

**Strengths:** *verus* is Latin for "true" — which maps directly onto the strongest differentiator in the whole product: *we never invent a number.* Of all the candidates, this one carries the most strategic meaning.

**Watch for:** "Vera" appears in several software and insurance contexts. Likely the hardest of the four to clear.

# Product vs. Company Naming

Consider separating the two, as the Constitution already hints ("Platform: AI Operations Platform / First Product: Bike Shop AI Copilot"):

| Layer | Approach |
|---|---|
| **Company** | Neutral, industry-agnostic — supports expansion beyond bike shops |
| **Product** | The chosen name (Tara/Nora/Orla/Vera) |
| **Business templates** | Descriptive, not separately branded — "Tara for Bike Shops", "Tara for Cafés" |

This avoids the trap of the "Bike Shop AI Copilot" naming, which would have to be abandoned the moment a café signs up (BD-002).

# Tone of Voice

**We sound like:** a knowledgeable colleague who respects your time.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Your margin fell 6% — here's what caused it" | "Leverage AI-powered insights to optimise performance" |
| "We can't tell you that yet — here's why" | Silently guess |
| "14 rows were skipped because they had no date" | "Import error: E_SCHEMA_VALIDATION_FAILED" |
| Short sentences. Concrete nouns. | Hype, superlatives, exclamation marks |
| "You" and "your shop" | "Users" and "entities" |

**Never:** claim the AI "thinks," "understands," or "knows" your business. It explains calculations. Saying otherwise undermines the one thing that differentiates us.

# Visual Identity Direction

Not final — direction for a designer.

**Feel:** clear, calm, trustworthy. Closer to a well-designed instrument panel than a consumer app.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Primary colour** | A deep, confident blue or teal — trust and clarity, distinct from the aggressive purples common to AI startups |
| **Accent** | One warm accent (amber or coral) for attention states and recommendations |
| **Semantic colours** | Green/amber/red reserved strictly for data states, never decoration |
| **Typography** | One clean humanist sans for UI (Inter, Source Sans); tabular figures mandatory for all numbers |
| **Logo** | Wordmark-led. Simple enough to work at favicon size. Avoid: brains, circuits, neural networks, glowing orbs |
| **Charts** | Restrained palette, colourblind-safe, no 3D, no gradients on data |
| **Imagery** | Real shops and real people where possible; avoid stock photography of robots |

**Deliberately avoiding:** the visual clichés of AI branding. The product's positioning is *trustworthy and grounded*, and glowing-neural-network imagery signals the opposite.

# Naming Process — Recommended Next Steps

1. **Shortlist two names** from the candidates above
2. **Preliminary self-check** — CRO, IPOI and EUIPO online search tools; domain availability for `.ie`, `.com`, `.eu`; social handles
3. **Say it out loud** — on a mock phone call. If it needs spelling twice, drop it
4. **Test with three bike shop owners** during Phase 1 interviews — ask which they'd trust
5. **Formal clearance** with a trademark agent before any spend
6. **Register** company name, domains, and social handles together, in one go
7. **File the trade mark** in the relevant classes (software/SaaS) before public launch

**Timing:** per `11_Development_Roadmap.md`, brand finalisation belongs in **Phase 5**, but domain and handle registration should happen as soon as a name is cleared — those get taken.

# Business Perspective

A name is cheap to change now and expensive to change after launch. The value of doing clearance properly is not the trademark itself — it is avoiding a forced rename after customers, domains, and printed material exist.

---

# Customer Perspective

The name should feel like something a shop owner would say casually: *"I checked Tara this morning."* If it sounds like enterprise software, it's wrong for this market.

---

# Current Decisions

* **LUMA is not recommended** — registered trademark conflict in the software class (Assessed)
* **EOLAS is not recommended** — crowded in Irish software and retail intelligence (Assessed)
* Company name and product name may be separated (Proposed)
* Brand finalisation belongs in Phase 5; domain registration immediately on clearance (Accepted)

# Risks

* Delaying naming too long means writing marketing material with a placeholder, which wastes effort.
* Registering domains before trademark clearance risks spending on a name that must be abandoned.
* All four candidates are real given names, which raises the chance of existing registrations in some class — clearance is not optional.
