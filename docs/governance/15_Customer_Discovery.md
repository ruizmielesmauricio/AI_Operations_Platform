# Document Contract

## Purpose

The purpose of this document is to validate the assumptions made in the Project Vision before significant development begins.

Rather than pitching our product, these sessions aim to understand how independent bike shops currently operate, identify their biggest operational challenges, and measure the value our future platform could provide.

# Executive Summary

The objective of every Customer Discovery Session is to understand the business—not to sell the solution.

We want customers to describe how they currently work, where they lose time, where they lose money, and which decisions they find most difficult.

The platform will be built around these findings.

---

# Interview Rules

Before every interview remember:

✅ Listen more than you speak.

✅ Ask "Why?" repeatedly.

✅ Let the owner tell stories.

✅ Ask for examples.

✅ Never defend your product idea.

✅ Never try to convince them.

✅ Validate problems before validating solutions.

---

# Section 1 – Understanding the Business

## Tell me about your shop.

* How long have you been operating?
* How many employees do you have?
* How many mechanics work here?
* How many locations do you operate?
* What brands do you sell?
* Approximately how many products do you stock?
* Which part of the business generates the most revenue?
* Which part generates the highest profit?

---

# Section 2 – Daily Workflow

Walk me through a normal working day.

Possible follow-up questions:

* What do you do first every morning?
* Which systems do you open?
* What information do you check?
* What reports do you use?
* Which tasks take the longest?
* Which tasks do you dislike the most?

---

# Section 3 – Inventory Management

* How do you decide what to reorder?
* How often do you place supplier orders?
* What information do you use?
* How do you know when stock is low?
* How often do you run out of products?
* Which products remain unsold for too long?
* How much inventory do you think is sitting on shelves unnecessarily?
* Which inventory decisions are the hardest?

---

# Section 4 – Workshop Operations

* How are repairs tracked?
* How are jobs assigned to mechanics?
* How do you know which repairs are completed?
* How do you know if mechanics are overloaded?
* How do you measure workshop performance?
* Which repairs generate the highest profit?
* Which repairs consume the most time?
* What delays workshop jobs the most?

---

# Section 5 – Financial Performance

* How do you monitor business performance?
* Which KPIs matter most?
* How do you know if profits are improving?
* When profits fall, how do you investigate why?
* How long does that process take?
* Which financial decisions are the most difficult?

---

# Section 6 – Suppliers

* How many suppliers do you work with?
* How do you choose suppliers?
* Do supplier lead times affect your business?
* Have supplier price increases affected profitability?
* What supplier information would you like to see more easily?

---

# Section 7 – Customers

* What do customers complain about most?
* Which products do customers request most often?
* Do customers often return products?
* How do you manage warranties?
* Which customer questions are repeatedly asked?

---

# Section 8 – Current Software

* Which POS system do you use?
* Do you use inventory software?
* Do you use accounting software?
* Do you use spreadsheets?
* Which software do you like?
* Which software frustrates you?
* If you could replace one system tomorrow, which would it be?

---

# Section 9 – Artificial Intelligence

* Have you used AI in your business?
* If yes, how?
* What concerns do you have about AI?
* Would you trust AI recommendations if they explained their reasoning?
* What would AI need to do before you trusted it?

---

# Section 10 – Decision Making

What are the hardest decisions you make?

Examples:

* Hiring staff
* Ordering stock
* Pricing
* Workshop scheduling
* Supplier selection
* Discounting products

For each decision ask:

* Why is this difficult?
* What information do you wish you had?

---

# Section 11 – The Magic Wand Question

If you could remove one frustration from your business tomorrow, what would it be?

Why?

How much time or money would solving that save?

---

# Section 12 – Looking Back

Tell me about the last business decision that turned out to be wrong.

Examples:

* Ordered too much stock
* Didn't order enough
* Missed a supplier opportunity
* Workshop delays
* Poor staffing decision

Ask:

* What happened?
* Why did it happen?
* Could better information have helped?

---

# Section 13 – Looking Forward

Imagine we're having this conversation one year from now.

What would make you say:

"Buying this platform was one of the best decisions I made."

# Success Criteria

This discovery phase will be considered complete when:

* At least 15–20 independent bike shops have been interviewed.
* The top 10 operational pain points have been identified.
* All assumptions in the Project Vision have been reviewed.
* The MVP scope has been prioritised using customer evidence rather than opinion.
